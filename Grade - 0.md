## 2. Resource Directory

### Structured Workbooks & Physical Curricula

* **Explode the Code Starter Primers (*Get Ready, Get Set, Go for the Code*):** Specifically created for Pre-K and Kindergarten learners. Focuses on lowercase letter forms, initial consonant sounds, and fine-motor tracing without visual overwhelm.
* **Bob Books (Stage 1 / Box Set 1):** Introduces only 4 sounds at a time (*m, a, t, s*), allowing children to read a complete book on day one with zero guessing.
* **Logic of English (Foundations A):** Multi-sensory phonics instruction linking letter sounds with gross-motor games and sandpaper letter tracing.
* **Kumon My Book of Rhyming Words & Alphabet Games:** Repetitive, tactile line-drawing and letter-sound association.

### Digital Phonics & Literacy Apps

* **Khan Academy Kids:** Free, comprehensive early literacy suite with interactive read-alouds, phonemic blending, and foundational tracing.
* **Teach Your Monster to Read (First Steps):** A structured game requiring children to feed monsters specific phonetic sounds, building letter-sound fluency without guessing.
* **Reading Eggs (Junior / Early Readers):** Structured animated lessons teaching phoneme segmentation and letter recognition.
* **Starfall ABCs:** Simple, unhurried interactive alphabet soundboard where touching each letter isolates its pure phonetic sound.
* **Duolingo ABC:** Gamified early reading and tracing focused on phonemic awareness and vocabulary.

## 5. Golden Rules for Preschool Phonics

1. **Teach Sounds, Not Just Letter Names:** Say "/b/" instead of "bee." Children cannot blend letter names (*"bee-ay-tee"* does not sound like *"bat"*).
2. **Prevent Guessing Early:** Never tell a child to look at the illustration to guess the word. Cover the picture with a sticky note, sound out the letters, and reveal the picture as a reward.
3. **Stop Before Frustration:** At the preschool age, 10–15 focused minutes of joyful practice outperforms an hour of forced drill work.

### Strategy, Chess & Logic Board Games

* **[Story Time Chess](https://codelearn.com/blog/10-board-games-to-stimulate-logical-and-mathematical-thinking/):** Uses illustrated, character-driven stories to teach how each chess piece moves (e.g., *King Shaky* only takes one nervous step at a time). It eliminates the dry memorization of traditional chess and lets kids as young as 3 or 4 play full mini-games on a real board.
* **Connect 4:** Pure forward planning and spatial awareness. It forces the child to think two moves ahead ("If I drop here, where will they drop?") without any language or math reading required.
* **Blokus (or Blokus Junior):** Players take turns fitting colorful geometric polyomino tiles on a grid corner-to-corner. It builds 2D spatial visualization, area perception, and geometry fundamentals.
* **Outfoxed!:** A cooperative detective logic game where players roll dice, search for clues, and use a tangible plastic evidence decoder to eliminate suspects using Boolean logic (e.g., "The fox is *not* wearing a scarf").

---

### Hands-on Math & Number Sense Games

* **Sum Swamp:** The gold standard introductory addition and subtraction board game. Players roll basic dice, add or subtract the numbers to move through a swamp loop, and practice odds vs. evens.
* **Tiny Polka Dot:** A deck of multi-colored cards showing numbers in various arrangements (dice dots, ten-frames, rings, and numerals). It trains *subitizing*—the crucial ability to recognize a quantity instantly without counting one-by-one.
* **Shut the Box:** A tactile wooden box with numbered tiles (1–9 or 1–12). Players roll two dice and flip down any combination of tiles that equals their roll, building number bonds and basic mental math.
* **Zingo! 1-2-3:** A fast-paced tile dispenser game that matches numerals to grouped object sets, perfect for early counting and quick number recognition.

## 1. Life Skills: Swimming & Water Competency

Preschool swimming prioritizes instinctual water safety, body autonomy, and self-rescue over competitive strokes.

### Safety Milestones

* **Breath Control (Blowing Bubbles):** Exhaling through the mouth/nose into the water prevents accidental inhalation and water aspiration.
* **The "Starfish" Back Float:** Teaches resting on top of the water to breathe and call for help without expending energy.
* **Wall "Monkey Walk" & Safe Exit:** Grasping the edge hand-over-hand to locate the stairs or push up and exit ("elbow, elbow, tummy, knee").
* **Turn-Around Recovery:** Stepping or falling into shallow water, immediately pivoting around 180 degrees, and grabbing the pool edge independently.

### Weekly Water Schedule

```
[2x per week / 30 mins each]
• 5 mins: Warm-up blowing bubbles, chin/face dips, and wall kicking.
• 10 mins: Assisted front & back floats (aiming for relaxed, belly-up posture).
• 10 mins: Wall jumps with immediate turn-around to grab the edge.
• 5 mins: Unstructured splash play to maintain positive water association.

```

## 2. Music: Rhythm, Pitch & Ear Training

Preschool music instruction is built on **internal beat awareness** and **pitch distinction** rather than technical sheet music reading.

### Essential Tools & Instruments

* **Wooden Rhythm Sticks (Lummi Sticks) or Egg Shakers:** Helps isolate steady beats, tempo changes (fast/slow), and rhythm echoes without mechanical complexity.
* **Color-Coded Handbells or 8-Note Resonator Bells:** Teaches high vs. low pitch associations and basic scalar movement (Do-Re-Mi) using color cues.
* **Prodigies Music (App / Web):** A video-led pitch and solfège curriculum using animated, color-coded bells to develop early relative pitch.

### 5-Minute Daily Music Games

1. **The Freeze Drum:** Beat a drum or clap hands continuously. When the drum stops, everyone freezes. Builds auditory processing and impulse control.
2. **High Bird, Low Bear:** Play a high note (reach hands to the sky like a bird) and a low note (touch toes or stomp like a bear) to solidify pitch recognition.
3. **Echo Rhythm:** Tap a simple 3-count pattern on rhythm sticks (`tap - tap - pause - tap`); the child taps it right back.

# Holistic Preschool Learning & Development Plan (Ages 3–5)

I would expand your existing plan beyond phonics, math, chess, music, and swimming. A preschooler's development should cover 10 interconnected areas, not just academic readiness.

The goal is to develop a child who can:

- Read simple words when developmentally ready.
- Understand numbers and solve everyday problems.
- Think logically and creatively.
- Communicate ideas and express emotions.
- Develop physical coordination and independence.
- Explore science, nature, and technology.
- Cooperate, share, and interact confidently with others.
- Develop curiosity and a lifelong interest in learning.

My recommendation is a play-based, structured learning system: short focused lessons, practical activities, physical play, reading together, and plenty of unstructured exploration.

## 3. Daily routine: A balanced learning day

I would not recommend 40 minutes of continuous academic instruction for a preschooler. Instead, spread learning across the day.

## Sample daily schedule

A flexible home-learning day; adjust around preschool, naps and family routines.

1. Morning – Language and literacy

   15 minutes
   - Read a story together.
   - Play a phonics or rhyming game.
   - Practice a few letter sounds.
2. Math and reasoning

   10–15 minutes
   - Count physical objects.
   - Play with number blocks.
   - Complete a puzzle.
3. Physical activity

   30–60 minutes or more

   \- Outdoor play, running, cycling or playground.
4. Creative exploration

   15–20 minutes

   \- Drawing, painting, LEGO or building.
5. Science or life skills

   10–15 minutes

   \- Nature exploration, cooking assistance or a small experiment.
6. Social play and games

   15–20 minutes

   \- Chess, Connect 4, pretend play or cooperative games.
7. Evening – Reading and music

   10–15 minutes
   - Parent-child story time.
   - Songs, rhymes or rhythm games.

The rest of the day should include meals, rest, free play, family interaction and sleep. Children should not feel that every activity is a lesson.

## 5. Age-based progression: What should a child learn?

A three-year-old and a five-year-old should not have the same curriculum. Use these as broad learning targets, not pass/fail tests.

## 7. Screen time and healthy development

I would change one thing in your original plan: digital apps should be optional reinforcement, not a daily requirement.

For ages 2–5, the AAP recommends no more than approximately one hour of high-quality screen media daily, with parents participating. Less is perfectly fine.&#x20;

[image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://publications.aap.org\&sz=32)

American Academy of Pediatrics+1



My suggested approach:

- 10–15 minutes of educational apps when useful.
- Prefer parent-child interaction over independent screen use.
- No screens during meals.
- Avoid screens for the hour before bedtime.
- Don't replace outdoor play, books or social interaction with apps.

For children under five, WHO also emphasizes adequate sleep, movement and limited sedentary screen time. For ages 3–4, its guidance includes 180 minutes of physical activity spread throughout the day and 10–13 hours of sleep.&#x20;
